# Mend release notes

Newest first. Downloads: https://github.com/JKS-sys/mend-08-oct-2026-releases/releases/latest · https://ipconfig.co.network/mend

## Mend 1.0.15 — 26 Oct 2026

- **Mend is now built for macOS on Apple silicon only** (M1 and newer). Intel Mac, Windows and Linux builds are no longer made or published, so releases build much faster.
- **No more Workers KV — the daily limit can no longer break Mend's server.** Computers saying hello and crash reports now go straight to the ipconfig accounts service (one ping per computer every 25 minutes at most), and activation codes and subscription links are kept in the accounts service's own store instead of Workers KV, which kept running into Cloudflare's free daily cap. The Owner Panel's Computers and Crashes tabs read from the accounts service and can still forget a computer or delete one or all crash reports; codes and subscriptions still appear on the dashboard every 15 minutes.
- **Disk Utility → + Volume checks a mistyped reserve or quota size first**, before it looks at your disks, so you get "that isn't a size" instead of a confusing "choose an APFS container" message.

## Mend 1.0.14 — 17 Oct 2026

- **Mount, unmount and eject work again — fixed at the cause.** On a Mac an external APFS drive shows its physical store partition, which can never be mounted, and Mend offered Mount for it; whole drives and containers had no Mount, Unmount or Eject at all; Eject of an APFS volume ejected the synthesized container instead of the drive; and a busy volume just failed. Now a store partition mounts its container, whole drives and containers get Mount all / Unmount all / Eject, Eject always ejects the physical drive (or the disk image), a busy volume offers **Who is using it?** and **Force unmount**, system and hidden partitions (EFI, Recovery, ISC, MSR) are no longer offered, snapshot ids like `disk3s1s1` are accepted, unmounted whole-disk sticks without a partition map appear, and the list refreshes after every job and every 5 s while the page is open (drives plugged in or mounted in Finder appear by themselves). Linux: unlocked LUKS/LVM volumes mount and unmount through their inner device, `mount`/`umount` are used when udisks2 is missing, and FreeBSD lists its disks with `gpart`. Windows: offline or read-only disks get **Bring online**, and folder mount points count as mounted.
- **Disks is now a full Disk Utility on Mac, Windows and Linux:** a Disk Utility-style toolbar (First Aid, Partition, Erase, Restore, Mount/Unmount, Eject, Info, + Volume, − Volume, Images ▾, RAID Assistant, View ▾) that stays one row from 420 to 1600 px; a sidebar grouped Internal / External / Disk Images with **Show Only Volumes / Show All Devices**; a usage bar (used, purgeable, other volumes, free) and partition map; **First Aid** on volumes → containers → the drive with a live log and advice; a searchable **Info** sheet with SMART status, encryption and APFS details; **Erase** with name, format, partition scheme (GUID / MBR / Apple), security passes for hard disks, encryption and a "which format should I choose?" helper; a **Partition** editor with a pie; **APFS** volumes with reserve, quota, role and encryption, **snapshots** (list and delete), Convert to APFS, Encrypt / Decrypt / Change password, Lock / Unlock; **disk images** (blank, from a folder, from a disk, open, convert, resize, verify, compact, make an ISO, change password); **Restore** from an image or another disk; a three-step **RAID Assistant** and RAID sets (add or remove members and spares, repair, auto-rebuild); journaling and ownership switches; troubleshooting steps with one-click fixes; right-click menus on every row and Disk Utility keyboard shortcuts. Passwords never appear in a command line or preview — they go to the tools on stdin.
- **Bad sectors and failing disks:** a **Bad sectors wizard** (find them in the drive's logs, the self-test log, the system log or a rescue map → confirm each one → rewrite or remap it per system → test again → report), a stronger **Failing disk** flow (stop the freezes, image the disk in gentle passes — fast, retry, backwards, last retries — with progress, or clone it straight to a new disk, then open the image and see what was lost), **disk errors in the system log** grouped per disk on Mac, Windows and Linux, a live health-score trend, and 53 more disk repairs grouped by what feels wrong: range self-tests, device statistics, cable-error reset, flaky USB enclosure fix (Linux), fake SD card check, eMMC wear, NVMe self-tests, RAID scrub and secure erase for SSDs.
- **184 more one-click repairs:** 131 for Mac, Windows, Linux, FreeBSD, iPhone/iPad and Android (Windows Defender repair and scans, Start menu repair, battery and Wi-Fi reports, blue-screen history; Mac Gatekeeper back on, login items reset, Mail index rebuild, kernel panic reports, Wi-Fi diagnostics; Linux pacman keys, firmware updates, GNOME/KDE fixes, SELinux relabel; FreeBSD now has its own repair page with base-system updates, pkg repair and boot environments; iPhone and Android "More repairs" with pairing reset, device clock, app profiles, app optimisation and A/B slot switching) plus the 53 disk repairs above. The Checkup now also checks Windows virus protection, firewall and pending restarts, Linux free space and FreeBSD ZFS pools.
- **The Fix Library grew to 19,880 fixes** (+2,308), many with a one-click repair; disk fix steps that were cut mid-sentence read correctly, and 214 disk fixes that were silently dropped as duplicates are back.
- **Log boxes everywhere are new:** a live status circle with progress, a running clock, colour-coded counts of steps, done lines, warnings and errors (click one to filter), search with highlights (press /), errors pinned at the top with Jump to and Ask Mend AI, follow-the-newest with a "Jump to latest" pill, wrap, line times, text size, a taller view, Copy and Save…, repeated lines folded with ×N and progress lines drawn as small bars. Every command output and crash report in the app uses it.
- **More colour in text and code (never pink):** disk names, paths, sizes, durations, dates, addresses, UUIDs, SMART names, flags and key=value pairs each get their own colour, and brackets are coloured by depth across eight hues. Each page and section has its own colour, and hint text is easier to read in both themes.
- **Right-click everything:** disks and volumes, devices, logs (copy only the problems, search, save, explain with AI), tables, pictures, links, selections, fixes, contacts, jobs, the status bar, the title bar and the empty background each get their own menu; menus fit small windows without scrolling and work with the arrow keys. The **Dock** and **menu-bar icon** menus gain Disk Utility, Disk Health, Recent Jobs and Appearance ▸ (theme, 16 layouts, sound packs, text size, animations). **Finder** gets First Aid, Disk Info, Eject, Unmount, Open or Verify a Disk Image, Make an ISO and Compress Folder to a Disk Image; Windows Explorer gets one tidy "Mend ▸" menu and the Linux file managers get the same verbs. The quick panel has 21 actions. Commands from the menu bar or the menu-bar icon no longer run twice.
- **Mend fits any window down to 420×260:** nothing overlaps, is cut off or scrolls sideways (checked on every page, tab and dialog at seven sizes, both themes and 1.5× text). **16 window layouts** in Settings → Appearance (Classic, Compact, Comfortable, Wide, Focus, Icon rail, Right sidebar, Top tabs, Bottom dock, Split view, Cards, Dense, Magazine, Studio, Minimal, Touch); the Fix Library has 12 views (new: Gallery, Split, Accordion, Kanban) and the Overview tiles and devices can be cards, list, tiles, compact or grid.
- **206 sounds, each with its own animated picture:** 58 new ones (mount, unmount, eject, First Aid, partition, erase, restore, burn, RAID, disk images, logs finishing, progress milestones, device plugged in by kind, theme sunrise/nightfall and more), smoother multi-colour animations with a soft glow, new disk pictures (a spinning hard disk, SSD chips, a USB stick, RAID sets, disk images), friendlier empty screens, and four new sound packs — Marimba, Synthwave, Kalimba and Lo-fi (14 in all). Small clicks show at most three pictures a second, and none in Subtle or Off.
- **The Owner Panel stays connected to the dashboard by itself — the Sync button is gone:** a live chip shows "Connected · synced 3 min ago" (or counts down while it reconnects; click to try now), the Worker pushes to the dashboard every 15 minutes even when nobody has the panel open, and every copy of Mend says hello from Rust every 30 minutes even with its window hidden or in the menu bar (Settings → Privacy still lets other people switch that off; the owner's Mac is always connected). The dashboard now gets full computer ids, where each crash happened and when each code was activated.
- **Smaller and faster to build:** unused Tauri and updater features are gone (about 160 KB less), the library is built once instead of three times, and Windows builds made with MinGW now link — so the Linux x64 and arm64 packages and the Windows x64 installer can be built on a Linux machine against an Ubuntu 22.04 sysroot (they run on Debian 12, Ubuntu 22.04 and newer). `update.sh` keeps Linux packages already copied into `release/` without starting a container.
- **Keyboard:** in Disks, ⌥⌘1 / ⌥⌘2 switch Show Only Volumes / Show All Devices (⌘1/⌘2 stay page navigation), ⌘I Info, ⌘E Eject, ⇧⌘E Erase, ⇧⌘P Partition, ⇧⌘R Restore, ⌥⌘O Open Disk Image, ⌘N New Blank Image, ⇧⌘N Image from Folder, ⌥⌘N Image from Volume, ⌘R Refresh; the Owner Panel moved to ⇧⌘O. No key is bound twice on any system (tested).
- **Safety and correctness fixes found in review:** an administrator script that could not start now never leaves an encryption password behind in its temporary folder; restoring onto one Linux partition unmounts only that partition (not `/boot` of the running system) and refuses to write if anything is still mounted; Raspberry Pi `/boot/firmware` and live-USB media count as the system disk; images are written as administrator only into real folders inside your home folder (no symlinks); Windows Eject sends the disk number and Explorer verbs on a drive root get the right path; per-disk commands from right-click, Dock and menu-bar menus act on the disk you clicked; `winmgmt` repairs run on Windows; MBR layouts stop at four partitions; RAID members are unmounted first.
- **Settings → Privacy** now says exactly what the anonymous hello sends and how often (every 30 minutes while Mend runs). The smallest window is now 420×300.

## Mend 1.0.13 — 15 Oct 2026

- **Releases build in a fraction of the time, all on the Mac, with Pour:** every Rust build cache now lives *outside* the project folder — the Mac and Windows targets in `/Users/Shared/mend-build/target` (`MEND_BUILD_CACHE=` to move it), Linux in the Docker volumes `mend-target-<arch>`, `mend-cargo-<arch>` and `mend-npm` — so a freshly unzipped source still builds incrementally (only Mend itself recompiles: about a minute per target instead of 8–10). The web app is built once and every `tauri build` reuses `dist/`. The two Linux builds run in the background while the Mac and Windows installers build (`--serial` to run them afterwards; the log is kept in `.release-cache/linux-build.log`). The build tools (nsis, llvm, docker, colima, node, gh…) are installed with **Pour** — Homebrew is never required; it is used only as a last resort when it happens to be present and Pour could not install something. Both GitHub repos get the full **20 topics** GitHub allows (`scripts/repo-details.sh` trims longer lists and warns about shorter ones).
- **Pour everywhere in the app too:** Helpers & Log installs libimobiledevice, ideviceinstaller, ipatool, smartmontools, Android platform-tools and pipx with `pour install` (Pour is installed first when it is missing — never Homebrew), the idevicerestore build script uses Pour's formulae, and Mend's search path includes Pour's bin folders (`POUR_PREFIX`, `~/.pour/bin`, `/opt/pour/bin`).
- **Dock + menu bar: closing the last window quits.** Whenever Mend is in the Dock — “Dock only” *or* “Dock and menu bar” — closing the last window or tab ends Mend completely, like any Mac app. Only “Menu bar only” and “Background” can keep running after the window closes (Settings says so next to the choice).
- **Settings → Updates:** the current version, **Check for updates now**, when the last check ran, and a switch for the check at start-up. A found update is also announced with a desktop notification.
- **The menu-bar icon has two pop-ups:** a **left click** opens a compact *quick panel* under the icon (connected devices, disks with their health, twelve one-click actions, Hide window, Quit; it closes when it loses focus or with Esc; a double-click opens the whole window) and a **right click** opens the full menu, now with a status line (devices · disks · lowest health), Quick Panel, *Quick Fixes* (flush DNS, repair the network, restart sound/Bluetooth/the desktop, rebuild search, permissions, fonts, icons, print queue, clock, system files, updates, disk/memory/sleep questions — each mapped to the right repair for this system), *Repair* (now with a short self-test on every disk and TRIM every SSD), *Back up*, *Contacts* (scan, AI tidy, export every account, import), *Mend AI*, *Appearance*, *Animations*, *Language*, *Mend Lives In*, *Window & Help* (show/hide, compact or large window, sidebar, check for updates, about, shortcuts, star on GitHub, logs, crash reports), Settings, Mend Pro and Quit. The same groups appear in the **Dock menu** and the native menu bar gets *Quick Fixes* and *Contacts* menus.
- **Finder gets seven more right-click Services:** Surface Scan, Save Disk Health Report, Tiny Backup Now, Free Up Space Here, Ask Mend AI About This, Install on iPhone (.ipa) and Restore iPhone from This Backup — next to the eight that were there. Windows Explorer and the Linux file managers get Tiny Backup Now, Save Disk Health Report, Free Up Space Here, Ask Mend AI and Install on iPhone too.
- **More notifications (only while Mend is not in front):** a repair or backup that ran longer than 20 s says when it finished or why it stopped (click opens the right page), a device plugged in while Mend is hidden says so, a device unplugged during a running job warns, and a new version announces itself.
- **Bad sectors and failing disks — more ways to repair:** *Rewrite bad sectors at an LBA* (zeros over just the sectors a self-test, the error log or a surface scan named, so the drive retires them — everything else stays), *Stop a failing disk freezing the computer* (SCT ERC 7 s), *Offline data collection* (refreshes the pending/reallocated counters), *Mark bad blocks in ext4 (keeps data)* (`e2fsck -c`), *Re-check bad clusters* (`CHKDSK /b`), *Deep repair* (`Repair-Volume -OfflineScanAndFix`), *Continue a rescue* from its ddrescue map and *Open a rescued image* read-only, *Read one sector by LBA*, *NVMe log* and *NVMe self-test*, Btrfs and ZFS scrubs, XFS and F2FS repairs, *Fix an NTFS volume on Linux* (`ntfsfix`), *Write caches to disk*, the ext bad-block list. Disk Health's rescue panel gained the matching steps; the Fix Library has eight more disk problems for every file system and media kind (≈ 1,000 new entries).
- **70 more software fixes:** macOS — Wi-Fi off/on, rebuild network settings, clear app caches, restart sharing, reset privacy permissions, rebuild the driver cache, reload login agents, Spotlight for one volume, swap usage, library cache (Intel), Quick Look previews, locate database, full Bluetooth reset. Windows — flush DNS, restart Explorer/sound/Bluetooth/network adapters, clear the print queue, rebuild font/icon/thumbnail caches, rebuild Search, clean temp files, repair WMI, sync the clock, reset BITS, re-register the Store and built-in apps, restore power plans, reset the firewall/hosts/proxy, repair Volume Shadow Copy, memory test. Linux — restart sound/Bluetooth/network, font cache, clock, trim logs, clean package caches, repair Flatpak, refresh snaps, clean temp, reset swap, regenerate locales, reset “open with”, clear GPU caches, check fstab, repair the package database. System Repair groups them by what feels broken.
- **Eight layouts for the Fix Library** — List, Cards, Compact, Tiles, Board (columns by category), Timeline, Magazine and Masonry — remembered between runs; an opened entry spreads across the row in every layout. Checked at 560, 640, 760 and 980 px: nothing overlaps or scrolls sideways.
- **Twenty new sounds with their own drawings** (bad sector, remap, scrub, freeze/thaw, quick panel, update found / up to date, layout, Finder, Dock, notification, quit, TRIM, self-test, Pour, cache, network, rewind) and a picture for every sound that had none (ticks, pops, pages, tabs, stars, steps, gauges, folders, clocks, keys…): 119 vector scenes for 131 sounds. Small UI sounds draw only in *Lively* and never more than a few a second. The colour audit still finds no hue between 280° and 350° anywhere.
- **Smaller start-up:** the owner panel and the quick panel are separate chunks loaded only by their own windows; the main bundle lost 150 KB.

- **The Linux AppImage ships again — packed without emulation:** linuxdeploy could not run under emulation on the Mac, so `scripts/appimage-cross.sh` builds the x86_64 AppImage natively from the amd64 `.deb` and the amd64 sysroot: every needed library found with readelf (linuxdeploy's exact exclude list), the tray-icon and SVG libraries it opens at run time, GTK input modules, pixbuf loaders, GIO modules and schemas with their caches, the WebKitGTK helper processes with the same relative-path patch Tauri applies, rpaths set with patchelf, squashed behind the official type-2 runtime and signed with the updater key. Verified on a clean Debian with no WebKitGTK installed: Mend and both WebKit processes run and the window renders.
- **iPhone backups that never finished are named as such:** a backup folder with Info.plist but no Manifest.db is an interrupted backup (Mend quit, cable out, phone locked), not “not an iPhone backup”. Data & export now says so with the state the backup recorded (Status.plist, read from binary plists too), marks it *incomplete* in the backup list, accepts the folder above a `<UDID>` folder, and offers **Back up again** right there — an interrupted backup resumes where it stopped.
- **iPhone battery health no longer shows 3 %:** newer iOS reports MaxCapacity/CurrentCapacity as percentages next to mAh values; dividing 100 % by the design mAh gave 3 %. Settings' own figure (MaximumCapacityPercent) is used when present, otherwise only capacities in the same unit as the design capacity, with a test for all three layouts.
- **Linux amd64 is cross-compiled now, not emulated:** one build image for the Mac's own architecture holds a full amd64 Debian sysroot (the GTK/WebKitGTK stack downloaded and unpacked under `/sysroot/amd64`, never executed) and the cross gcc; cargo links against it with `--sysroot`, so the amd64 `.deb` builds at native speed in a few minutes instead of 70+ under QEMU, where gcc crashed. Only the AppImage packer (linuxdeploy) still needs emulation and stays best-effort. Tested here with the mirror-image setup (x86_64 host → arm64 sysroot): a signed `Mend_arm64.deb` in 2m45s, ARM binary, correct NEEDED libraries and Depends.
- **amd64 Linux under Rosetta, not QEMU:** the first amd64 container build took 72 minutes under QEMU and its AppImage packer (linuxdeploy) failed. update.sh now switches Rosetta on itself — colima is restarted with `--vm-type vz --vz-rosetta`, Docker Desktop gets “Use Rosetta for x86_64/amd64 emulation” turned on and is restarted — and the AppImage is packed in a second, verbose step with `NO_STRIP`; if it still fails the `.deb` is published and latest.json keeps the previous AppImage instead of the whole release stopping.
- **Homebrew's docker CLI has no BuildKit:** `docker build` fell back to the legacy builder and rejected `--progress`. update.sh now installs `docker-buildx` and links it as a CLI plugin (Docker Desktop users already have it), and only passes `--progress=plain` when buildx is there. Finished Windows/Linux installers for the current version now survive a rerun (release/ is no longer wiped whole).
- **Linux builds on the Mac, fixed and tested:** the build packages are now installed once into a reusable image (`scripts/linux-build.Dockerfile` + `scripts/linux-deps.sh`) instead of on every run; apt's real error is printed when something fails (it was hidden behind `dpkg returned an error code (1)`), with an automatic repair-and-retry and a workaround for the emulator crash that most often breaks amd64 package installs on Apple silicon; Node.js falls back to the official nodejs.org build if NodeSource fails; the container gets `CI=true` (the Tauri CLI rejects `CI=1`); arm64 builds first (native, fast); each architecture builds into its own folder with a kept Rust cache and crate volume, so reruns are quick; installers already built for this version are kept on a rerun.
- **Windows ARM64 build on a Mac:** `ring` assembles its ARM64 Windows code with plain `clang`, which rejects the clang-cl `/imsvc <dir>` header flags cargo-xwin sets (“no such file or directory: '/imsvc'”). update.sh now puts a tiny clang shim first in PATH during the Windows builds that rewrites `/imsvc` to `-isystem`.
- **Windows build on a Mac whose home folder has a space:** cargo-xwin keeps the Windows SDK under `~/Library/Caches/cargo-xwin` and joins its library paths into RUSTFLAGS with spaces, so a home like `/Users/Jagadeesh Kumar S` failed with “flag in rustflags must not contain its separator”. update.sh now keeps that cache at `/Users/Shared/cargo-xwin` (moving an already-downloaded SDK there) — `XWIN_CACHE_DIR` overrides it.
- **Every installer from the Mac, no GitHub Actions:** `update.sh` now installs what the Windows and Linux builds need when it is missing (cargo-xwin, NSIS, LLVM; Docker Desktop if present, otherwise colima + the docker CLI, started headless with Rosetta) and stops the release if a Windows or Linux build fails instead of quietly leaving it to CI. The CI workflow runs only with `--ci`; `--mac-only` publishes just the Mac builds on purpose. (The 1.0.10 run shipped only the DMGs because cargo-xwin, makensis and Docker were not on the Mac.)
- **Menu bar drop-downs:** new **Devices** (every connected phone/tablet, refresh, restart the USB service, back up now) and **Disks** (every disk with its health, full report, save a report for every disk, Disks & Partitions, duplicates) menus that follow what is connected; **Repair** and **Back Up** menus; View → Animations and View → Language (15 languages); Window → Mend Lives In (Dock / menu bar / background) and a test notification; Help → Star Mend on GitHub, Install with Pour (copies the two lines), Open the Log Folder, Helpers & Log.
- **`bash update.sh` no longer stops at the Rust tests on a Mac where Mend is running:** the single-instance test connected to the real `~/Mend/mend-<uid>.sock`, handed its arguments to the running app and reported a failure. It now uses a temporary socket (and a second test proves the hand-over round trip). Nothing had been published on 14 Oct because of it; the next run goes through.
- **Contacts → AI tidy (new tab):** short, meaningful names (drops emojis, numbers, "work/new/old", repeated words and shouting, keeps Tamil/Hindi/Arabic script) and notes summarised to two lines — proposed by the AI Mend already uses (a local model on the Mac keeps everything on the computer; the built-in free one otherwise), shown as before → after rows you tick before anything is saved. - **Disk Health judged against real drives:** laptop and USB hard disks are rated on 300,000 start-stop cycles, not 50,000, and a worn counter never drags the estimate below 20 % by itself — only damage does (a WD10SPCX with 119,000 start-stops now reads about 60 % left, not 0 %). New findings: heads parking many times an hour (→ APM 254), spinning down all day (→ standby off), minutes spent over the temperature limit, thousands of CRC errors flagged as cable/bridge. Thousands of CRC errors cost at most 8 points.

### Install

- **macOS on Apple silicon** (M1 and newer): `curl -fsSL https://ipconfig.co.network/pour/install | sh` then `pour install --cask mend`

First launch on macOS after a manual DMG install: `xattr -cr /Applications/Mend.app` (Mend is ad-hoc signed, not notarized).
